# Top-Down Lab

The runnable demo for Lessons 16–25: a six-legged creature walking a circuit on a
movement plane, past a patch of ground it is not allowed to stand on.

Open `Scenes/TopDownLab.unity` and press play.

## What you are looking at

| Drawing | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Cyan chain + green arrow | The body and its heading. The arrow is `PlanarHeading`, which rotates leg homes — it does not turn the body. |
| Gold rings | Each leg's heading-relative home. Watch them swing around the body as it turns (Lesson 16). |
| Gold stars | The candidate fan the planar query offered this tick. Turns red when none of a leg's options are legal (Lesson 17). |
| Blue / amber legs | Planted and swinging. Amber legs come in threes — the alternating tripod (Lesson 22). |
| Dark cross vs. bright ring | A foot's shadow sits on its committed planar point; the ring is the same point pushed up the screen by the lift curve (Lesson 19). |
| Red body | Gait has nowhere legal to step and has asked locomotion for help (Lesson 25). |

## Who owns what

- `TopDownIntentSystem` steers the circuit, keeps the **body** out of the blocked
  region, and is the only thing that acts on `GaitRecoveryRequest` — it slows down
  and takes the turn gait suggested. Body obstacle response is locomotion's job:
  a creature that walks its own centre into an obstacle leaves every leg with
  nowhere to go, and no foot policy can rescue that.
- `PlanarQuerySystem` reports `Walkable` and `PathClear` for a fan of points around
  each leg's predicted home. It ranks nothing and never writes a plant. Replace it
  with a tilemap, navmesh, or physics query and nothing else changes.
- `TopDownLabDemo` draws everything above from resolved output. Delete it and the
  simulation is identical.
- The creature itself is composed from the package's authoring components. This
  project constructs no plants, swing state, or chain points.

`Scenes/` is generated by `TopDownLabSceneBuilder` (menu:
**Tealeaf → Rebuild Top-Down Lab Scenes**), so the setup is reviewable as code
rather than as scene YAML.

## Why the authoring assembly is not editor-only

`Tealeaf.ProceduralAnimation.Dots.Editor` deliberately carries no
`"includePlatforms": ["Editor"]`. Authoring MonoBehaviours have to be runtime
scripts even though they only ever live in a sub-scene: Unity refuses to attach an
editor-only script to a GameObject, and the sub-scene importer strips one it finds
in a scene, leaving an entity with no `VerletChain`:

```text
GameObject (named 'Top-Down Creature') references runtime script in scene file. Fixing!
The referenced script is missing on Top-Down Creature (index 5 in components list)
```

That is what stopped this scene and `Lab.unity` from baking while the flag was
present. Bakers compiling into a runtime assembly is the standard Unity DOTS
arrangement, and nothing in that folder uses the `UnityEditor` namespace.
