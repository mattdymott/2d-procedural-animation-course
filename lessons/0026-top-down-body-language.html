Top-Down Body Language: Bank After the Turn

A top-down creature can turn correctly yet feel like a cardboard cutout. Let the rendered body bank and stretch from resolved motion—but do it after gait, IK, and constraints have already made the turn legal.

Filter the picture, not the path

Recipe · derive a filtered render bank
// Read-only simulation evidence.
float turnRate = SignedAngle(previousForward, resolvedForward) / deltaTime;
float bankTarget = clamp(-turnRate * visual.BankGain,
                         -visual.MaxBank, visual.MaxBank);

// Presentation-only second-order state.
visual.Bank = UpdateSecondOrder(visual.Bank, bankTarget, deltaTime);
float stretch = 1f + length(resolvedVelocity) * visual.StretchGain;

// Render output only.
render.Rotation = HeadingAngle(resolvedForward) + visual.Bank;
render.Scale = new float2(stretch, 1f / stretch);

The target turn still belongs to locomotion and gait. The body bank is merely a delayed interpretation of that turn, so it may overshoot or settle without changing a planted foot or a planar candidate.

Top-down visual signals

SignalDerived fromMeaning
BankFiltered turn rateWeight shifting into or out of a turn.
Longitudinal stretchResolved planar speedCommitment and acceleration.
Follow-through offsetFiltered body motionMass lagging behind the legal root.
Foot lift/shadowCurrent swing stateLegibility of an existing step.

These signals can share a presentation filter but need not share a simulation component. Keep the render adapter free to choose sprite rotation, mesh deformation, or camera-facing offsets without teaching gait any of those details.

DOTS mapping

A presentation system reads the resolved simulation after the fixed solve group and evolves its own second-order state. It publishes a render-facing pose or managed bridge. The fixed-step gait systems never query that output.

public struct TopDownVisualFollow : IComponentData
{
    public float Bank, BankVelocity;
    public float Stretch;
}
