Stop and Go: Weight Shift Without Sliding Feet

A top-down creature that stops instantly can feel massless. A small filtered render offset lets the body drift into a brake and lag into acceleration, while planted feet and collision stay exactly where the simulation put them.

Acceleration is a picture input

Recipe · filter a render-only weight shift
float2 acceleration = (resolvedVelocity - previousVelocity) / deltaTime;
float2 shiftTarget = -math.normalizesafe(acceleration)
                   * visual.WeightShiftDistance;

// Presentation-only state.
visual.Offset = UpdateSecondOrder(visual.Offset, shiftTarget, deltaTime);

// Draw after fixed simulation resolves.
render.Position = Project(resolvedBodyPosition) + visual.Offset;

On braking, the body picture drifts forward briefly; on acceleration, it lags behind. The legal body point, heading, and planted targets do not move because of the effect. If they need to change, locomotion or gait must do it through their normal rules.
