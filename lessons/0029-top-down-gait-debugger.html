Top-Down Gait Debugger: See the Plant Slip

A planted foot is a committed world point. This little debugger makes the contract visible: rotate or move the creature and its homes move, while its plants stay put until a completed swing deliberately replaces them.

Run the experiment

Turn the heading toward 90 degrees with world-pinned plants. Gold homes rotate with the creature; pale plant dots remain in the world, so the dashed stress lines grow. Then enable the red “drag plants” bug. The lines falsely vanish because the feet are skating with the body.

World-pinned plants preserve the evidence that a step is needed.

Gold rings are heading-relative homes. Pale dots are committed plant points. A red dashed line is over comfort; that is a gait reason to request a swing, not permission to move a planted dot.

☐ Challenge 1 — Reveal a legal step request. With the bug off, turn past 75 degrees or add speed until at least one stress line is red.
☐ Challenge 2 — Catch the skating lie. Turn with stress visible, then enable “drag plants.” Explain why the now-short lines are evidence of the bug, not a stable gait.

What “commit” means in the real gait

The button is a controlled shortcut: it sets every plant to its current home so you can see stress clear. A live solver must not do that each tick. It asks for a candidate, swings toward it, then updates PlantedWorldPoint only when that swing lands.

Recipe · keep query, promise, and picture separate
// Gait reads stress from a persistent world promise.
float stress = distance(foot.PlantedWorldPoint, headingRelativeHome);

// Candidate selection is a transition, not a planted-tick overwrite.
if (foot.Phase == Swing && Reached(foot.CandidateWorldPoint))
{
    foot.PlantedWorldPoint = foot.CandidateWorldPoint;
    foot.Phase = Planted;
}

// Presentation may lift or shadow the rendered foot.
// It never changes PlantedWorldPoint.
