Top-Down Moving Supports: A Foot Follows Material

A top-down conveyor or turntable does not challenge the gait state machine. It challenges the coordinate frame of a planted promise. Keep the plant relative to the support and, when necessary, to material travelling through it.

Support movement is planar movement

In the top-down branch, supports translate and rotate on the same XY/XZ plane as body locomotion. Lesson 19’s raised sprite is irrelevant here: a conveyor changes the planar target; visual lift only makes a swing between targets readable.

Recipe · evaluate an existing relative promise
// At the instant gait commits a landing:
leg.Support = hit.Support;
leg.LocalPlant = InversePlanarTransform(hit.Support, hit.Point);
leg.SurfaceOffset = 0;

// Every planted tick — no fresh foothold query:
leg.SurfaceOffset += support.BeltVelocityLocal * deltaTime;
float2 localPoint = leg.LocalPlant + leg.SurfaceOffset;
leg.WorldPlant = PlanarTransformPoint(leg.Support, localPoint);

// At liftoff:
float2 carry = VelocityAtPoint(leg.Support, localPoint)
             + PlanarTransformDirection(leg.Support, support.BeltVelocityLocal);

A translating platform needs the support entity and local plant. A stationary conveyor adds the changing local surface offset. A rotating conveyor needs both terms. In every case, the support adapter evaluates a relation that gait already chose.

For a turntable, use the world plant’s radius from the pivot: point velocity equals linear velocity plus angular velocity times perpendicular radius. The support centre’s velocity is incomplete whenever the support rotates.

DOTS mapping

Keep the support relation beside gait state, but make the adapter resolve it before IK. Locomotion consumes carry only at the gait liftoff handoff. Presentation reads the resolved world plant and may still render a separate swing lift afterward.

public struct PlanarSupportPlant
{
    public Entity Support;
    public float2 LocalPlant;
    public float2 SurfaceOffset;
    public byte IsRelative;
}

Where to go deeper

Primary source: Unity’s Rigidbody2D.GetPointVelocity documents the important liftoff principle: velocity at a contact point includes angular motion, not merely the body centre’s linear velocity. Apply that point-velocity idea on your chosen top-down movement plane.

Keep the top-down moving supports cheatsheet beside the earlier moving-platform, conveyor, and rotating-support references.

Suggested real-world rep: place an L9 creature on a stationary XY conveyor, then on a rotating belt. Draw LocalPlant, SurfaceOffset, WorldPlant, and the liftoff carry arrow. Verify that gait decides release but never recomputes the planted target from a fresh hit.

Ask about support destruction, slipping policies, or a top-down creature with a gripping hand rather than a planted foot.
